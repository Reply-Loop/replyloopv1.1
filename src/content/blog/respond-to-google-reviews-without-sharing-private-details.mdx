A family takes the time to write a Google review about your agency. Some are warm. Some are hard to read. Either way, the review is public, and so is your reply. The next family choosing an agency will read both.

For most local businesses, a reply is simple: thank the person, mention something specific, invite them back. Home care is different. The person writing may be a client, a family member, or someone who only asked about your services. A reply that sounds kind can still say more than it should.

This guide walks through how to answer every review in a way that is warm, honest, and private.

## Start with one rule

**A public reply never confirms that the reviewer, or anyone they mention, was your client.**

That holds even when the reviewer says so first. A reviewer can share their own story. Your reply does not have to repeat it, and it should not add to it.

Hold to that rule and most of the hard choices make themselves.

## What a public reply can say

A good reply in home care is short and general. It can:

- Thank the person for taking the time to write.
- Say that their words mean something to your team.
- Share public facts about your agency that you would put on your website anyway.
- Invite them to call the office if they would like to talk.

That is enough. A reply does not need detail to feel sincere. Sincerity comes from tone, from answering every review, and from answering the same way for everyone.

## What a public reply should never say

Leave these out of every public reply, even when the review mentions them:

- Whether the reviewer or their family member was a client.
- A visit, a schedule, or an appointment.
- A diagnosis, a condition, or a treatment.
- A caregiver's name, or anything that happened between staff and a client.
- Billing, payment, or insurance.

A reply that names a family member, thanks someone for a specific visit, or mentions how a client is doing confirms the relationship in public. The reviewer chose what to share about themselves. The agency does not get to choose for them.

## Two example replies

These are examples written for this guide. They are not from a real agency or a real review.

**Example, not a client.** A reply to a kind review:

> Thank you for taking the time to write this. It means a lot to our whole team. If there is ever anything you would like to talk through, please call the office.

**Example, not a client.** A reply to a critical review:

> Thank you for sharing this. We take every concern seriously and would like to understand more. Please call the office so we can talk directly.

Notice what both replies leave out. Neither confirms a relationship. Neither mentions care. Both point the real conversation to the phone.

## Hard reviews: slow down and move the conversation

A critical review can sting, especially when you know the full story and the reviewer has told only part of it. The public page is not the place to tell the rest.

A few habits help:

- **Wait before replying.** Read it, step away, and come back. A reply to a review of 3 stars or lower should wait until the owner has seen it.
- **Do not argue the facts in public.** Even a correction can confirm details you should keep private.
- **Keep it short.** Thank them, take the concern seriously, and invite a call.
- **Follow up for real.** The reply is only an invitation. The call is where the problem gets solved.

Future families are reading your reply more than the reviewer is. A calm, short answer tells them you listen and that you protect people's privacy, even under pressure.

## Ask every customer the same way

Replies are only half of the picture. The other half is how reviews arrive.

Ask every client family for a review the same way, with the same link and the same words. Do not ask only the families you expect to be happy. Do not offer anything in return for a review, good or bad. No gift, no discount, no favor.

That keeps your reviews honest. Over time, honest reviews, answered well, become something your agency can stand on.

## A checklist before you post a reply

Run through this before a reply goes live:

1. Does it avoid confirming the reviewer or anyone they named was a client?
2. Does it leave out visits, conditions, caregivers, billing, and insurance?
3. Does it use only general thanks and public facts about your agency?
4. If the review is hard, does it invite a call instead of answering in public?
5. Has the owner seen it, if the review is 3 stars or lower?
6. Would you be comfortable if the reviewer, the next family, and your staff all read it?

If every answer is yes, post it.
