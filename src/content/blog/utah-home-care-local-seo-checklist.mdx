Local SEO sounds technical. For a home care agency in Utah, most of it is not. It comes down to a few plain questions: can a family nearby find you on Google, does what they find match the truth, and does it give them a reason to call?

This checklist covers the parts an owner or administrator can understand and check without a marketing degree. Work through it once, then keep the monthly section going.

## Part 1: Your Google Business Profile

Your Google Business Profile is your listing on Google Search and Maps. For many families, it is the first thing they see.

- **Use your real business name.** Write it the way it appears on your signage and paperwork. Do not add cities or keywords to the name.
- **Choose an accurate primary category.** Pick the category that best describes your agency. Add secondary categories only if they are true.
- **Set your service area honestly.** If clients receive services at their homes and you do not see visitors at your office, Google lets you hide your street address and list the areas you serve. List the cities and counties you truly cover, such as Salt Lake City, Murray, or the rest of Salt Lake County.
- **Keep hours and phone current.** Use the phone number a family should actually call. Make sure it matches your website exactly.
- **Write a plain description.** Say who you serve, where, and how to reach you. Skip claims you cannot back up.
- **Add real photos.** Your office, your team with their permission, your vehicles. Never use photos of clients.
- **Link to the right page.** Point the website link to a page that makes calling easy.

## Part 2: Your reviews

Reviews do more than add stars. They show a family how you treat people, and your replies show whether anyone is listening.

- **Ask every client family the same way.** Use one review link and one simple ask for everyone. Do not choose who gets asked based on how you think they feel.
- **Never reward a review.** No gift, discount, or favor in exchange for a review, good or bad. Google does not allow it, and it erodes the trust your reviews are meant to build.
- **Reply to every review.** Thank people for the kind ones. Answer the hard ones calmly and invite a call.
- **Keep replies private.** A public reply should never confirm that the reviewer or anyone they mention was your client, and should never mention care, visits, billing, or insurance. Our guide on [responding to Google reviews without sharing private details](/blog/respond-to-google-reviews-without-sharing-private-details) goes deeper.
- **Let the owner see low-star reviews first.** A reply to a review of 3 stars or lower should wait until the owner has read it.

## Part 3: Your website

Your website and your Google profile should tell the same story.

- **Match your name, phone, and service area.** The same wording on your site and your profile helps Google and families trust both.
- **Make the next step obvious.** Every page should have one clear way to reach you. On a phone, the number should be one tap away.
- **Describe where you serve in plain words.** One honest page about your service area is better than a stack of near-copies for every city.
- **Load quickly on a phone.** Many families will find you on their phone. Test your own site on yours.
- **Answer the questions families ask.** Short, general answers to common questions help a family decide to call. Keep them general and never describe a real client.

## Part 4: What to check each month

Local SEO is not a one-time project. A short monthly routine keeps your profile active and accurate.

1. **Read and reply to every new review.**
2. **Post on your Google profile.** Share news, hiring, community involvement, or a plain answer to a common question.
3. **Check your profile details.** Google sometimes applies changes suggested by the public. Make sure your name, hours, phone, and service area are still correct.
4. **Look at your profile as a family would.** Search for your agency on your phone. Is anything missing, out of date, or confusing?
5. **Keep Facebook and Instagram in step.** The same news, adapted for each place, keeps your agency from looking quiet.
6. **Write down what changed.** A short note each month makes it easy to see progress over time.

## What to skip

Some advice does more harm than good:

- Stuffing city names into your business name.
- Buying reviews, trading reviews, or asking staff to post them.
- Creating thin pages for every town you could possibly reach.
- Promising rankings. No one controls where Google places you.

Honest, steady work tends to hold up. Shortcuts tend not to.
