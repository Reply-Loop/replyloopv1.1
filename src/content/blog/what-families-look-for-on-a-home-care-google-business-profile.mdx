Picture an adult child looking for home care for a parent. They are worried, busy, and trying to make a good choice quickly. They search on their phone, and a short list of agencies appears on Google Maps.

Before they call anyone, they look. What they find on your Google Business Profile shapes whether your agency makes the shortlist.

This guide walks through what a family can see on your profile, what each part tells them, and what you can do about it. None of it requires a large budget. It requires care and a steady routine.

## Your reviews, and whether you answer them

Reviews are often the first thing a family reads closely. They are looking for how other families felt, in their own words.

They also notice your replies, or the lack of them. A profile full of kind reviews with no answers can feel like no one is home. A profile where the owner answers every review, warmly and briefly, tells a family that someone is paying attention.

**What you can do:**

- Reply to every review, kind or critical.
- Keep replies short, warm, and general.
- Never confirm in a reply that the reviewer or their family member was your client, and never mention care, visits, billing, or insurance.
- For a hard review, take the concern seriously and invite a call to the office.

Our guide on [responding without sharing private details](/blog/respond-to-google-reviews-without-sharing-private-details) has example replies.

## The tone of your replies

Families read tone as carefully as they read words. A defensive reply to a critical review can worry a family more than the review itself. A calm, respectful reply can reassure them.

**What you can do:**

- Wait before replying to a hard review. Let the owner read it first.
- Do not argue details in public.
- Sound like a person, not a template. A reply in the owner's own voice reads very differently from a canned line.

## Recent activity

A profile that has not changed in a long time can make a family wonder whether the agency is still active. Recent posts and recent replies show a living business.

**What you can do:**

- Post on your Google profile regularly: agency news, hiring, community events, or plain answers to common questions.
- Keep Facebook and Instagram in step, so a family who looks further finds the same active agency.

## Accurate basics

A family needs to know a few simple things fast: Do you serve their area? Are you open? How do they reach you?

**What you can do:**

- List your real service area. If clients receive services at their homes and you do not see visitors at your office, Google lets you hide your street address and list the areas you serve instead.
- Keep your phone number and hours current.
- Make sure the website link goes to a page with a clear way to call.
- Check your profile each month. Google can apply edits suggested by the public, and wrong details cost calls.

## Photos that feel real

Photos help a family picture your agency. Stock images and empty profiles do not.

**What you can do:**

- Add photos of your office, your team with their permission, and your vehicles.
- Never post photos of clients or their homes.
- Replace old photos when things change.

## A description that answers the first questions

Your profile description is a short space to say who you are. Families skim it for the basics.

**What you can do:**

- Say who you serve, where, and how to reach you.
- Use plain words, not industry terms.
- Leave out claims you cannot back up, such as rankings or promises about outcomes.

## How the family decides to call

Put together, a family is asking a few quiet questions as they look at your profile:

- Do other families speak well of this agency?
- Does the owner listen and answer?
- Is this agency active and real?
- Do they serve where my parent lives?
- How do I reach them?

A profile that answers all five, honestly, earns the call. You do not need to be the biggest agency in Salt Lake County. You need to be clear, current, and attentive.
